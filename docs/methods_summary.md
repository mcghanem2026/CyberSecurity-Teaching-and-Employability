# Methods summary (see the paper, Section 3, for the full account)

1. **Design.** Pragmatic, mixed-methods documentary analysis (Bowen, 2009) of the *intended* curriculum
   (van den Akker's intended / implemented / attained distinction). No human participants; public documents only.
2. **Corpus.** 51 NCSC-certified programme records: 41 master's (MSc/MSci/MEng), 10 bachelor's/integrated (exploratory);
   15 at Russell Group providers, 36 elsewhere (heterogeneous residual group; exploratory comparison only).
3. **Mapping.** Credit apportioned across the five CyBOK v1.1 broad categories in proportion to documented module content;
   general-introductory credit set aside; the five substantive shares re-normalised to sum to one. Content on the
   security of AI systems — for which CyBOK v1.1 has no category — could only be apportioned to existing technical
   categories or set aside as foundational (a limitation discussed in the paper, §5.4 and §6.4).
4. **Breadth.** Herfindahl–Hirschman Index over the five re-normalised shares; descriptive only.
5. **Reference points.** (a) Certification profiles from the CyBOK Mapping Booklet v2.2; (b) a Big Four demand proxy
   built from the certification profiles and a role-family-weighted skills dictionary. Gaps = proxy − mean supply.
6. **Rigour.** Consistent protocol; audit trail; independent double-coding of a 10–25% subset (O'Connor & Joffe, 2020);
   triangulation across internal patterns, certification profiles and the proxy; negative cases examined; sensitivity
   check retaining introductory credit as a sixth category left the findings unchanged.
7. **Interpretation boundary.** The mapping measures documented knowledge content (human capital) only; it does not
   measure social, cultural, identity or psychological capital, and equity implications are advanced as theoretically
   grounded associations, not causal claims.
